Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before your journey to the Himalayas.

Contact us immediately if you need to cancel and we'll guide you through the process.

All multi-day packages (2-D/1-N and above) include accommodation and select meals as detailed on each package card. Day-trip packages include a guide and transport but not meals unless specified.

✈️ Travel & Visas
Do I need a visa to visit Nepal?
+
Most nationalities require a tourist visa for Nepal. Visas can be obtained on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport (Kathmandu) or at major land border crossings, and are also available through the official Nepal eVisa portal. Indian nationals do not require a visa.
What permits are needed for trekking?
+
Trekking permits depend on your destination:
  • TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System) — required for most trekking routes.
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) — for Annapurna region treks.
  • Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit — for Everest Base Camp treks.
All permits are arranged by us as part of your package.
Will I have a guide throughout the tour?
+
Yes. All our packages include an experienced, English-speaking licensed guide. For multi-day treks, a porter can also be arranged on request.
What is the best time of year to visit Nepal?
+
October–November (autumn) and March–May (spring) are the best seasons — clear skies, mild temperatures, and great mountain views. Winter (Dec–Feb) is cold but beautiful and less crowded. The monsoon season (June–September) sees heavy rainfall and is generally not recommended for trekking.
🏥 Safety & Health
Is Nepal safe for tourists?
+
Nepal is generally a safe and welcoming destination for tourists. As with any travel, we advise staying aware of your surroundings, keeping valuables secure, and following your guide's instructions in remote areas.
What vaccinations should I get before travelling to Nepal?
+
We recommend consulting your doctor or a travel health clinic. Common vaccinations include Hepatitis A & B, Typhoid, Tetanus, and Rabies (for extended stays). No vaccination is legally required for entry unless you are arriving from a yellow fever–endemic country.
What is altitude sickness and how can I prevent it?
+
Altitude sickness (AMS) can affect trekkers above 2,500 m. Symptoms include headache, nausea, and dizziness. Prevention tips:
  • Ascend gradually — no more than 300–500 m of altitude gain per day above 3,000 m.
  • Stay well hydrated.
  • Avoid alcohol during ascent.
  • Consult your doctor about Diamox (acetazolamide) before the trip.
Our guides are trained in altitude sickness first aid and will monitor your condition throughout.
Do I need travel insurance?
+
Travel insurance is strongly recommended and required for all trekking packages. Ensure your policy covers high-altitude trekking, emergency medical evacuation (helicopter rescue), and trip cancellation.
🎒 Packing & Weather
What should I pack for a trek in Nepal?
+
Essentials include:
  • Moisture-wicking base layers and warm mid-layers (fleece/down jacket).
  • Waterproof outer shell jacket and pants.
  • Comfortable, broken-in trekking boots and warm socks.
  • Sunscreen (SPF 50+), sunglasses, and a wide-brim hat.
  • Reusable water bottle and water purification tablets.
  • Basic first aid kit and personal medications.
  • Headlamp with spare batteries.
Gear can also be rented affordably in Kathmandu's Thamel district.
What is the weather like in Kathmandu and Pokhara?
+
Kathmandu and Pokhara sit at around 1,300–900 m altitude. Expect warm days (20–28°C) and cooler nights (8–15°C) during peak seasons. Winter months (Dec–Feb) can drop to near freezing at night. Always carry a light jacket regardless of the season.
Can I buy or rent gear locally in Nepal?
+
Yes — Thamel in Kathmandu is a hub for outdoor gear shops. Trekking boots, sleeping bags, trekking poles, and down jackets are widely available for rent or purchase at very reasonable prices.
